What am I looking at?

The spacetime

You sit on a point mass $M$ in 2+1-dimensional de Sitter space. The geometry is exact (Vaidya–de Sitter):

$$\begin{gathered} ds^2 = -f\,du^2 - 2\,du\,dr + r^2 d\phi^2,\\ f = 1 - \frac{8GM(u)}{c^2} - \frac{r^2}{\ell^2}, \end{gathered}$$

with $r$ the areal radius. In 2+1 dimensions a point mass makes a cone, not a black hole, and your horizon sits at $r_c = \ell\sqrt{1-8GM/c^2}$.

Lengths are in $\ell$, times in $\ell/c$, masses in $c^2/8G$ (the largest possible mass).

The sky

Each object is drawn where it was when it sent the light reaching you now. Its colour is the wavelength you receive, computed exactly through the changing geometry, and it fades as the light reddens. Galaxies look flattened along your line of sight, as light from their near and far ends implies.

  • Dotted circle, $r=\ell$: the horizon of empty de Sitter, reached once you have given away all your mass.
  • Dashed circle, $r_c$: the apparent horizon for your present mass. It jumps outward when you launch.
  • Solid circle: the visible edge, the farthest point that the light reaching you now came from. It catches up with $r_c$ over a few $\ell/c$.

Launching

Each tap sends out a shell of 12 beacons at $0.999c$, carrying $\Delta M$ of your mass. The galaxies came from you too: before the game started you launched them the same way, so you begin with a horizon that is still growing.

The Penrose diagram

Light moves at 45°. Gold is your past, everything you have seen; blue is your future, everything you can still reach. The solid pink line is the event horizon (if you launch nothing more), the dashed pink line the apparent horizon, stepping out at each launch, and the small pink ring the visible edge. Now keeps the present in view; Full shows the whole history.

Things to notice

  • Everything reddens by a factor $e$ per $\ell/c$ of your time, whatever your mass. The mass only sets how big the horizon is.
  • Images pile up at the horizon and never cross it; each beacon has a last message you will ever receive.
  • Launching makes everything beyond the shell look slightly bluer: its light gains energy crossing the shell on its way to you.
